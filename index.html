01 / ABOUT

Field experience.
Crop-focused questions.

I study how crop and nutrient management decisions shape agricultural productivity, bringing together field experimentation, laboratory analysis, and farmer engagement.

At Penn State, I work with Dr. Heather Karsten to characterize wood- and poultry litter-derived biochars and evaluate their effects on ammonia volatilization, nitrous oxide emissions, soil fertility, nutrient cycling, and corn silage yield in no-till dairy cropping systems. My background spans participatory rice variety trials, seed production, and agricultural advisory services in Nepal.

I am seeking Ph.D. opportunities primarily in agronomy and crop science, with interests in precision agriculture, crop improvement, and nutrient management. My long-term goal is applied research with practical value for growers and the agricultural industry.

03 / RESEARCH

From field trials
to better decisions.

Current work and foundations for future research.

Penn State · Current research

Biochar characterization, soil fertility & corn silage production

My two-year field and laboratory project evaluates wood- and poultry litter-derived biochars in no-till dairy cropping systems, examining ammonia volatilization, nitrous oxide emissions, soil nutrient dynamics, and corn silage yield.

I manage field operations and soil/crop sampling, measure nitrogen losses, characterize biochar, and analyze treatment responses using mixed models, repeated measures, and regression.

Field experimentationNutrient managementCrop productivity

Undergraduate research · LI-BIRD internship · February–July 2023

Participatory selection of spring rice

I conducted this undergraduate research for Far Western University during my internship with Local Initiatives for Biodiversity, Research and Development (LI-BIRD), Nepal, under the Sowing Diversity = Harvesting Security (SD=HS) project. I evaluated twelve spring rice genotypes with farmers in Kailali, combining experimental design, crop management, field observations, and farmer participation in varietal evaluation.

06 / METHODS & ENGAGEMENT

Tools, teaching
& outreach.

Field & analytical methods

Research plot establishment; RCBD and split-plot designs; corn and cover crop management; crop scouting; soil and manure sampling; and participatory on-farm trials.

Biochar & soil analysis

Hands-on experience with the Gasmet FTIR GT5000 for N₂O measurements, semi-open chambers and acid traps for ammonia volatilization, flow injection analysis (FIA) for soil NH₄⁺ and NO₃⁻, and the Elementar UNICUBE CHNS analyzer for biochar composition. Additional work includes X-ray fluorescence (XRF), electrical conductivity (EC), ash determination, soil moisture and temperature monitoring, and laboratory quality assurance.

Data & spatial tools

R, JMP, mixed-effects models, ANOVA/ANCOVA, repeated-measures analysis, regression, data visualization, QGIS, RTK GPS, and nitrogen-cycle modeling.

Teaching & extension

Teaching assistant for Plants and Humans (Spring 2026) and Ornamental Plant Materials (Fall 2026). Outreach includes Penn State Extension’s FOCCE webinar series and Ag Progress Days.
